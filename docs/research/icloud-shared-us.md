# What iCloud does on its own when the board writes to the shared Us calendar

Research note, 2026-10-30, for fork issue [#65](https://github.com/bellamy1506/HA-Family-Hub-Clover/issues/65) (map [#37](https://github.com/bellamy1506/HA-Family-Hub-Clover/issues/37)). Question: once Us is iCloud's Home calendar, renamed and shared with Jenn ([#41](https://github.com/bellamy1506/HA-Family-Hub-Clover/issues/41)), and the board and Clover write to it through Home Assistant's CalDAV sign-in (the owner's Apple Account), what do Apple's servers and phones do by themselves? Not repeated here: writing to iCloud ([icloud-editing.md](https://github.com/bellamy1506/HA-Family-Hub-Clover/blob/research/icloud-editing/docs/research/icloud-editing.md)) and who added what inside Home Assistant ([who-added.md](https://github.com/bellamy1506/HA-Family-Hub-Clover/blob/research/who-added/docs/research/who-added.md)).

Lines marked **[inference]** are my own judgement; **(unverified)** marks what no source here confirms. Sources: Apple's iPhone User Guide for iOS 27, iPad User Guide for iPadOS 27, Calendar User Guide for macOS 27 and iCloud User Guide, all read 2026-10-30; Apple's own CalDAV extension specs and its open-source Calendar Server ([apple/ccs-calendarserver](https://github.com/apple/ccs-calendarserver), last commit `13c706b`, 2020-02-12); Home Assistant 2026.10.4's `components/caldav` and the `caldav` 3.3.0a1 library it pins; Apple Community threads, as evidence of experience only. Nothing touched iCloud, Home Assistant or a phone.

## The answer

Apple's default is to push. When anyone changes a shared iCloud calendar, iCloud tells everyone else on it: an iPhone notification (Shared Calendar Changes, on until turned off), a Calendar inbox entry that waits for an OK, and optionally an email. The notice names the Apple Account that made the change, and every board, wall and Clover write reaches iCloud as the owner's account, so Jenn's phone would announce each one as Will's, including the adds she makes herself by voice. The owner's own phone should stay quiet for those, because Apple's notification spec forbids notifying people of their own changes (iCloud itself wasn't tested). Each phone can turn the notices off, for Us alone where the calendar's Show Changes switch still exists or for all shared calendars in Settings, which leaves the house board's new dot as the only "something changed" signal, as notice-don't-push wants. Event alerts are separate and per person. Jenn needs an Apple Account with iCloud Calendar turned on and an invitation with editing allowed; changes reach phones by push. Home Assistant would list a calendar shared to the owner's account, but that doesn't matter here: the owner owns Us, and `calendar.home` keeps its entity id through the rename and the share.

## 1. What iCloud sends when Us changes

- **iPhone notifications, on by default.** "When someone modifies a calendar you're sharing, you're notified of the change. You can turn off notifications, if you don't want to receive them": Settings > Notifications > Calendar > Customize Notifications > Shared Calendar Changes ([Share iCloud calendars on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/share-icloud-calendars-iph7613c4fb/ios), iOS 27). The iPadOS 27 guide says the same ([iPad](https://support.apple.com/guide/ipad/ipadc2a14a22/ipados)).
- **A per-calendar switch, older.** The iOS 15 guide put it on the calendar itself: Calendars, the info button next to the shared calendar, turn off **Show Changes** ([iPod touch, iOS 15](https://support.apple.com/guide/ipod-touch/iph7613c4fb/ios)). The iPhone guide has given only the Settings path since iOS 16 (versions 16, 17, 18, 26 and 27 read). Users still described Show Changes, under "shared events" on the calendar's info page, in January 2023 (iOS version not given) and October 2024 (an iPhone 7, which stops at iOS 15); the 2024 post adds that event alerts kept coming until turned off further down the same page (Apple Community [254541955](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/254541955), [255801960](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255801960); experience). Whether iOS 27 still shows it: (unverified).
- **Calendar's inbox.** Each change also waits in Calendar's inbox for an OK on the other person's phone; the OK only dismisses it ("Why do we have to OK changes made by others", iOS 18, 2025-03-04, answered by a Level 7 user pointing to the Settings switch: [255995757](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255995757); also the 2024 post above; experience).
- **Email, optional.** On iPhone: Settings > [name] > iCloud > See All > iCloud Calendar > **Receive Email Updates**, "an email when a change is made to events on a calendar that has been shared with you" ([Send and receive invitations for iCloud Calendar](https://support.apple.com/guide/icloud/send-receive-invitations-icloud-calendar-mm4a440a7b64/icloud)). iCloud.com offers the same as "Email me shared calendar updates" ([Receive updates to calendars](https://support.apple.com/guide/icloud/receive-emails-updates-shared-calendars-mm8074582205/icloud)) and as a choice between in-app notifications and email ([Share a calendar on iCloud.com](https://support.apple.com/guide/icloud/share-a-calendar-mm6b1a9479/icloud)); the choice applies to every device on that Apple Account. Defaults aren't stated (unverified).
- **Mac.** Calendar > Settings > Alerts > "Show shared calendar messages in Notification Center"; "If you deselect this option, you still receive notices in Calendar or by email" ([Alerts settings, macOS 27](https://support.apple.com/guide/calendar/icl40727/mac)). The house records list no Mac.
- **Made on the server.** iCloud can email these updates, so it produces them on its server, whatever app made the change **[inference]**. Apple's own extension defines them ([CalDAV: Calendar User Notifications](https://github.com/apple/ccs-calendarserver/blob/master/doc/Extensions/caldav-notifications.txt), Daboo, Apple, 2012-03-19, section 5.3): one notification per created, updated or deleted event, carrying `CS:changed-by` with the changer's first and last name (or display name) and account; "servers MUST NOT generate notifications to users for changes they themselves make, though the possibility of an automated process acting on behalf of a user needs to be considered"; and a `CS:notify-changes` property that each user sets per calendar to turn them off (section 5.3.4), which looks like what Show Changes sets **[inference]**. Apple's open-source Calendar Server never implemented these (no `resource-change` or `notify-changes` code at `13c706b`), so iCloud's closed server is the only implementation: the spec shows Apple's design, not proof of iCloud's behavior.

## 2. Whose name it shows

- **Every Home Assistant write is the owner's.** The CalDAV integration builds one `DAVClient` from the config entry's username and password (`caldav/__init__.py`, `async_setup_entry`, [2026.10.4](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/blob/2026.10.4/homeassistant/components/caldav/__init__.py)). The wall's +, a phone's board, and "tell the board" from either iPhone all reach iCloud as the owner's Apple Account, so Jenn's notices would name Will for all of them, her own voice adds included **[inference from the spec]**.
- **The owner's phone stays quiet for those** (the spec's MUST NOT), and gets notices only for changes made on Jenn's iPhone **[inference; untested on iCloud]**.
- **Calendar shows no "added by".** No Apple guide describes a creator on an event, and an August 2025 question, "Is there a way to show who created an event on a shared apple calendar" (macOS 15.5), went unanswered ([256111257](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256111257)). The notice and the inbox entry are the only places Apple names anyone; their exact wording is (unverified).
- **A board add is not an invitation.** Home Assistant's create path sends only title, start, end, description, location and repeat rule, never attendees (`calendar.py`, `async_create_event`, [2026.10.4](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/blob/2026.10.4/homeassistant/components/caldav/calendar.py)): a plain event Jenn sees because she's on the calendar, nothing to accept. An invitation has attendees, asks Accept, Maybe or Decline ([Reply to invitations](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/reply-to-invitations-iphc0eddfe3c/ios)), and lands in each person's default calendar for "Events & Invitations" ([Choose the default calendar](https://support.apple.com/guide/icloud/choose-a-default-calendar-mmbc29b09053/icloud)).

## 3. Event alerts: separate, and per person

- **Alerts aren't shared.** "When you share a calendar, the alerts do not get shared as well. Each person a Calendar is shared to can set up an alert" (Apple Community Specialist, 2022-05-05, [253873425](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/253873425)). Apple's Calendar Server stores alarms per user on shared calendars ([`peruserdata.py`](https://github.com/apple/ccs-calendarserver/blob/master/twistedcaldav/datafilters/peruserdata.py): `VALARM` inside `X-CALENDARSERVER-PERUSER`).
- **Default alerts reach board adds.** On a Mac, default alerts "are applied to new and existing events that you haven't set an alert for," on every device unless kept to that computer ([Manage notifications](https://support.apple.com/guide/calendar/icl624fd1912/mac), [Alerts settings](https://support.apple.com/guide/calendar/icl40727/mac)); on iPhone, Settings > Apps > Calendar > Default Alert Times ([Change Calendar settings](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/change-calendar-settings-iphc37be2016/ios)). Calendar Server gives a new event without an alarm the calendar owner's default (`X-APPLE-DEFAULT-ALARM`), but "never add default alarms to calendar data in shared calendars" when the writer isn't the owner ([`sql.py`](https://github.com/apple/ccs-calendarserver/blob/master/txdav/caldav/datastore/sql.py), `processAlarms`). So a board add would remind each of you at your own default time, if you've set one **[inference]**.
- **Off for one calendar:** Calendars, info button, Event Alerts ([Use multiple calendars](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/use-multiple-calendars-iph3d1110d4/ios)). These remind you of the event itself, which is why it's on the phone; they aren't change notices.

## 4. What Jenn needs

- **An Apple Account that uses iCloud:** "When you share a calendar privately, your invitees must have an Apple Account and use iCloud in order to use the calendar" ([Share a calendar on iCloud.com](https://support.apple.com/guide/icloud/share-a-calendar-mm6b1a9479/icloud)). She has one: she accepted the Apple Home invite (J006, 2026-10-29).
- **iCloud Calendar on her iPhone:** Settings > [name] > iCloud > See All > iCloud Calendar > Use on this iPhone ([Keep track of events](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/keep-track-of-events-iphdafdf98a1/ios)). Whether it's on now isn't recorded; her calendar is her Gmail.
- **An invitation to her email:** the owner shares from Calendar (Calendars, info button next to Home, Add Person); "your contacts must have an email address" ([iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/share-icloud-calendars-iph7613c4fb/ios)). She gets "a notification and an email" and can accept on "your iOS or iPadOS device that's set up for iCloud in the same Apple Account, or on iCloud Calendar on iCloud.com" ([Share calendars on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/calendar/icl32362/mac); [Accept on iCloud.com](https://support.apple.com/guide/icloud/accept-a-shared-calendar-invitation-mm6b1a88ad/icloud)). Invitations to Gmail addresses arrive (2022, [254338561](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/254338561); experience); the safe choice is the address her Apple Account uses **[inference]**.
- **Editing allowed:** "Allow editing: Allows the invitee to create, edit, and delete events," versus "Read-Only" ([iCloud.com](https://support.apple.com/guide/icloud/share-a-calendar-mm6b1a9479/icloud)); on iPhone, the person's Allow Editing switch. "Only the calendar's owner can share a private calendar," so only the owner can change access later.
- **Her default stays hers.** Joining adds Us to her calendar list; her default calendar, a phone setting, stays Gmail.

## 5. How fast changes travel

- **Board to phones: push.** "If you choose Push, the account is updated automatically when you make a change on any of your devices that use the same account, or when someone makes a change to a shared calendar in that account" ([Refresh calendars on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/calendar/refresh-calendars-icl1024/mac)). On iPhone a Fetch schedule is only for accounts "that don't support Push" ([Change Calendar settings](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/change-calendar-settings-iphc37be2016/ios)). Apple gives no figure; seconds is typical **[inference, unverified]**. Low Power Mode turns off email fetch and Background App Refresh, and doesn't list calendar push ([Apple, 2026-09-21](https://support.apple.com/en-us/101604)).
- **Phones to board: about a minute.** The board asks every 60 seconds and the CalDAV integration reads iCloud live (J008; icloud-editing.md).

## 6. Home Assistant and calendars shared to an account

- **It would list them.** Home Assistant asks the account's calendar home for its calendars (`api.py`, `async_get_calendars`: `client.get_principal().get_calendars()`); `caldav` 3.3.0a1 keeps every child whose type is a CalDAV calendar (`collection.py`, `_is_calendar_resource`), and Home Assistant keeps those that hold events and have a name. Apple's sharing extension puts an accepted shared calendar "in their own calendar home collection (i.e., alongside their own personal calendars)" ([Shared and Published Calendars in CalDAV](https://github.com/apple/ccs-calendarserver/blob/master/doc/Extensions/caldav-sharing.txt), Daboo and York, Apple, 2012-09-19, section 3). So yes **[inference: iCloud follows its spec]**.
- **It doesn't matter here.** The owner owns Us, already `calendar.home`. It would matter for option D below (a house account that is only invited to Us), or if Jenn ever shared an iCloud calendar of her own with the owner: a new entity would appear at the next reload.
- **The rename keeps the entity.** The unique id is `{entry_id}-{calendar.id}` (`calendar.py`, `async_setup_entry`), and `calendar.id` is the last part of the calendar's address (`collection.py`, `_extract_calendar_id_from_url`), not its name. Renaming Home to Us changes the entity's name at the next reload, not `calendar.home`. Sharing doesn't move the owner's calendar: the sharee gets a new collection pointing at it (sharing spec, section 3) **[inference for iCloud]**.

## Implications for the house board

1. **What Apple's change notices do on each iPhone once Us is shared** (for the owner, at the switch-over fog). The rule is notice, don't push; Apple's default pushes every change to the other person.
   - **A.** Off on both phones: Show Changes off for Us where the calendar's info page has it, otherwise Shared Calendar Changes off in Settings; Receive Email Updates off; event alerts left as each person likes. The house board's new dot is the one change signal, and it names people correctly. **0.65**
   - **B.** Off on Jenn's, on on the owner's: the owner hears each change Jenn makes from her phone; Jenn hears nothing. **0.10**
   - **C.** Apple's defaults on both: Jenn's phone announces every board, wall and voice add as Will's, her own voice adds included; the owner hears Jenn's phone edits; each change also gets a dot. **0.10**
   - **D.** A house Apple Account ("Clover") that Home Assistant signs in with, invited to Us with editing, notices on: Apple's notices say Clover instead of Will. Costs a second Apple Account to keep (two-factor phone number, app-specific password, recovery), a second CalDAV sign-in, and a push to both phones for every board add. **0.15**

   **Recommendation: A.** It keeps the rule and gives both adults the same quiet phones and one honest signal, for two switches per phone. It gives up hearing about a change while away from the board; that waits for the dot. D alone makes Apple's credit honest, at the price of an account to maintain and more pushes. Jenn's input isn't assumed (map notes); her switch stays hers to turn back on.
2. **Check the owner's default calendar before sharing** (technical call; the owner can veto). If Home is the default (iPhone Settings > Apps > Calendar > Default Calendar; iCloud.com "Add Events & Invitations to"), then after the switch his iPhone adds, Siri's adds and invitations he accepts land on Us, in Jenn's view. #41 leaves defaults to each person; this makes sure the choice is made knowingly. Will's calendar is the natural default.
3. **Look over Home's older events before sharing** (technical call; the owner can veto). #41 read August 2026 to January 2027 only; Jenn will see the whole calendar, past included.
4. **Switch-over phone steps** (technical call; the owner can veto): rename Home to Us; share it from the owner's iPhone to the email of Jenn's Apple Account with Allow Editing on and Public Calendar off; Jenn checks iCloud Calendar is on, accepts, and keeps her default; then the settings from item 1. Phone steps, not Home Assistant changes.
5. **Nothing changes in Home Assistant for the rename or the share** (technical call; the owner can veto). `calendar.home` keeps its id and the house board's person assignment; its name reads Us after the next CalDAV reload, which is cosmetic.
6. **One check during the switch-over** (technical call; the owner can veto): add a made-up event from the wall, look at both phones (banner, Calendar inbox, email, alert), then Undo it. Two minutes; it settles the open points below on the real account and phones.
7. **The new dot stays as decided in #61** (technical call; the owner can veto). Apple's notices can't feed it (Home Assistant copies eight fields from iCloud and none is an author) and credit Will for every board add, so Family Hub's "last touched by" stays the only author source. A phone change to Us reaches the board within about a minute; a board change reaches the phones within seconds.

## What this says not to do

- Don't let Apple's notices serve as the household's change signal: they credit Will for everything Clover writes.
- Don't share Us read-only (Jenn couldn't fix things from her phone) or as a public calendar.
- Don't add attendees to board events, or invite Jenn to events in place of sharing the calendar: invitations ask for answers and send messages.
- Don't read iCloud's notification collection to learn who changed Us from a phone: iCloud's version of the 2012 extension is closed and undocumented, and it would read the owner's own notices **[inference]**.
- Don't try to silence Jenn's notices from Home Assistant: `notify-changes` is set per person, so the owner's sign-in can only change the owner's **[inference from section 5.3.4]**.
