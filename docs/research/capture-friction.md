# Capture friction: the seconds between "this came up" and "it's on the board"

Research note, 2026-10-29. Question: at the moment an obligation appears (a text, an email, a phone call, a paper flyer, a conversation, a thought in the car), what makes people actually record it on a shared calendar or list, what stops them, and which capture channels and interaction patterns bring the time and emotional cost close to zero for a two-adult iPhone household with Home Assistant and a Claude-backed voice assistant? Lens: the seconds between "this came up" and "it's on the board", including the obligations nobody wants to think about.

This note builds on [skylight-calendar.md](skylight-calendar.md) (Skylight's Magic Import by photo, voice and email), [family-display-ecosystem.md](family-display-ecosystem.md) (the market's forward-to-an-address pattern, receipts with per-item undo, the "new since you last looked" dot) and [delight-and-discoverability.md](delight-and-discoverability.md) (undo instead of confirms, echoing what landed, tapping the day to add). It doesn't repeat them; where it agrees it says so in a line, and where it corrects one it says why.

Lines marked **[inference]** are my own judgement or arithmetic, not something a source states. Platform versions are given with each capability: iOS 26 (September 2025); iOS 27, released 2026-09-14 with "Siri AI" in beta; Home Assistant core 2026.10 (core's development branch reads 2026.12.0.dev0 on 2026-10-29, so 2026.11 is in beta); Home Assistant iOS app 2026.10.3 (2026-10-29).

**Method note.** Pages were found with WebSearch and opened with WebFetch; PDFs were read by extracting their text; Home Assistant core facts were read from its source on GitHub (development branch, 2026-10-29). The session's shared WebSearch budget ran out partway, so a few claims rest on a search summary or abstract (marked "via search"). See Gaps.

## 1. The mechanisms that decide whether something gets captured

### 1.1 At the moment it comes up, ability decides, not motivation

- **Fogg Behavior Model:** "Behavior happens when Motivation, Ability, and a Prompt come together at the same time"; "Without a Prompt, the target behavior will not happen"; "Simplicity is a function of your scarcest resource at that moment" ([behaviormodel.org](https://behaviormodel.org/), [ability](https://behaviormodel.org/ability/), [prompts](https://behaviormodel.org/prompts/), fetched 2026-10-29; first published at Persuasive 2009). **Evidence quality:** a design framework, not a tested predictive model; the peer-reviewed tests found are narrow (condom use; a health behaviour in Nigeria, Pakistan and India; via search, [PMC9414643](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9414643)).
- **Effort cuts offloading, measured:** Chiu & Gilbert, two pre-registered experiments ([QJEP 2023, doi 10.1177/17470218231199977](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10176285/1/chiu-gilbert-2023-express-influence-of-the-physical-effort-of-reminder-setting-on-strategic-offloading-of-delayed.pdf), read in full): "At all memory loads, reminder-setting was reduced when it was more effortful." Low-effort reminders let people reach "near-perfect accuracies"; higher effort weakened that, and the effect of effort was largest at **intermediate** memory loads, which the authors read as "when participants are uncertain about their strategy choice." Their discussion cites Grinschgl et al. 2020 (Computers in Human Behavior 108): less offloading with mouse than touch, and with a less responsive interface.
- **[inference]** For capture, the arrival of the obligation is the prompt, and it's already there. What varies is ability (free hands, attention, which app is open) and motivation (low for anything dreaded). Design for ability. The items lost first are the middling ones ("renew the registration sometime") that aren't urgent enough to force the effort and aren't trivial enough to drop.

### 1.2 People offload only to a store they trust

- **Risko & Gilbert 2016** ([Trends in Cognitive Sciences 20(9) 676-688](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1508770/), via search): the decision to offload rests on beliefs about whether it's more efficient than memory, and those beliefs can be wrong.
- **Weis & Wiese 2019** ([Human Factors 61(2) 243-254](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0018720818797553), abstract via search): *believed* reliability, not only actual reliability, set how much people offloaded; telling people a tool was less reliable than it was cut offloading substantially.
- **People over-remind rather than under-remind:** Gilbert and colleagues found a stable bias toward setting more reminders than optimal, tracking underconfidence in memory ([UCL, "optimal reminders"](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10080846/), via search); fixing the confidence didn't remove it ([Engeler & Gilbert, PLoS ONE 2020](https://doaj.org/article/9e77dc6fb7e040f7913d1d6b25187f19), via search). Self-initiated offloading raises the chance an intention is carried out ([Gilbert, QJEP 68(5) 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25404057/), via search).
- **Evidence quality:** careful lab tasks, partly pre-registered; the jump to a household calendar is **[inference]**.
- **[inference]** Willingness isn't the bottleneck; people like to offload. Captures are lost to effort and to doubt. One silent failure ("I said it, it never showed up") teaches that the board might not have it, and every later capture then costs capture plus checking.

### 1.3 "It's handled": plans quiet the mind; a bare note may not

- **Masicampo & Baumeister 2011** ([JPSP 101(4) 667-683, doi 10.1037/a0024192](https://supp.apa.org/psycarticles/supplemental/a0024192/a0024192_supp.html), abstract via search): unfinished goals caused intrusive thoughts, raised accessibility of goal words and hurt an unrelated task; "letting participants formulate specific plans for their unfulfilled goals eliminated" those effects, mediated by "the earnestness of participants' plans."
- **Replication:** none found in this search. The memory effect it builds on didn't hold up: a 2025 meta-analysis "found no memory advantage for unfinished tasks" but did find a general tendency to resume them ([Ghibellini & Meier, Humanities and Social Sciences Communications 12, 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html)).
- **Bedtime to-do lists:** 57 adults, polysomnography; writing a to-do list before bed led to falling asleep about 9 minutes faster than writing about completed tasks; no neutral control group ([Scullin et al., JEP: General 147(1) 139-146, 2018](https://news.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2018/can-writing-your-dos-help-you-doze-baylor-study-suggests-jotting-down-tasks-can), via search).
- **Getting Things Done:** "capture" is step one of five (capture, clarify, organize, reflect, engage), on the claim that the mind is for having ideas, not holding them. Its scientific case is a theoretical review from distributed cognition ([Heylighen & Vidal, Long Range Planning 41(6) 585-605, 2008](https://web-archive.southampton.ac.uk/cogprints.org/6289/)); no controlled trial was found. GTD depends on a weekly review to empty the inbox.
- **[inference]** Treat "it's handled" as the design goal, not a measured effect. What survives the weak evidence: relief comes from the item being somewhere trusted *with a when*. A "dentist" note that walks forward every day is a reminder, not a plan.

### 1.4 When-and-where plans help, but less than the famous number

- **Meta-analyses:** d = 0.65 over 94 tests ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes)). The 2025 update over 642 tests: effective for cognitive, affective and behavioural outcomes, ".27 ≤ d ≤ .66", larger with an if-then format, high motivation and rehearsed plans ([Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer, European Review of Social Psychology 36(1) 162-194](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/handle/123456789/69905)). Secondary sites report that bias corrections in that paper shrink the average a lot (one method to d ≈ 0.15); **not checked against the paper**.
- **Field:** a flu-shot mailing that asked people to write down the *date and time* they'd go raised vaccination by 4.2 points over a 33.1% control; *date only* gave 1.5 points, not significant; 3,272 employees ([Milkman et al., PNAS 2011](https://www.nber.org/papers/w17183), via search).
- **Defaults:** a pre-scheduled flu-shot appointment (opt out) reached 45% against 33% for opt in, 239 per arm ([Chapman et al., JAMA 304(1) 2010](https://behavioralpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/chapman-web.pdf), via search); a later trial in health-care workers found no detectable effect on vaccination, only on booking (Lehmann et al., Vaccine 2016, via search).
- **[inference]** For dreaded items: a concrete time beats a date, and a proposed default beats a blank. The motivation moderator means plans work least for exactly these items, so a default slot does more than asking people to plan.

### 1.5 Avoidance: the dreaded item is aversive to capture and to look at later

- **Task aversiveness** is among the strong, consistent predictors of procrastination (691 correlations; [Steel, Psychological Bulletin 133(1) 65-94, 2007](https://prism.ucalgary.ca/handle/1880/47914), via search).
- **Procrastination as mood repair:** people put off what makes them feel bad now, and the future self pays ([Sirois & Pychyl, Social and Personality Psychology Compass 7(2) 115-127, 2013](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/91793), via search).
- **The ostrich problem:** people avoid information about their own progress, especially when it implies bad news or effort ([Webb, Chang & Benn, Social and Personality Psychology Compass, 2013](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/76946/), via search); in a follow-up, believing the information inaccurate or not useful predicted avoidance ([Chang, Webb & Benn, Frontiers in Psychology 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5297323)).
- **Doctor avoidance, national data:** of 7,674 US adults (HINTS 2008), about 30% avoid the doctor even when they suspect they should. Of 1,369 coded reasons: traditional barriers 58.4% (cost 24.1%, **time 15.6%**), unfavourable views of care 33.3% (fear, embarrassment or guilt 5.6%), low perceived need 12.2%, "lazy" or "procrastinate" about 3.3% ([Taber, Leyva & Persoskie, JGIM 30(3) 290-297, 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4351276)).
- **[inference]** Two rules follow. (1) Capture mustn't make anyone dwell: "dentist" is a complete entry, with no questions about duration, who, or which calendar. (2) The board mustn't become something to avoid looking at: no growing red piles, no counts of what wasn't done. And since time is a real barrier, a bounded "Call the dentist, 10 min, Wednesday 12:30" is easier to face than an open-ended "deal with dentist."

### 1.6 Obligations arrive away from the calendar, and whoever transcribes becomes the keeper

- **Family calendars** (44 families, interviews in their homes; [Neustaedter, Brush & Greenberg, ACM TOCHI 16(1) art. 6, 2009](https://grouplab.cpsc.ucalgary.ca/grouplab/uploads/Publications/Publications/2009-CalendarCrucial.TOCHI.pdf), read in full): in 41 of 44 families the mother was the primary scheduler; most primary calendars were paper on the kitchen wall or fridge. "Family members find out about calendar changes throughout the day, and people are not necessarily at the calendar to update it." Workarounds: phoning home and leaving the details on the answering machine to copy later; phoning the children to write it on the calendar; appointment cards copied at home; family members writing sticky notes for the primary scheduler "to transcribe later." Batch updates came with notices (the school year), and keeping several calendars in step was "painful," with fear of merging work and family calendars.
- **A digital kitchen calendar in the field** (4 families, 4 weeks; Neustaedter, Brush & Greenberg, Graphics Interface 2007, read in full): a public spot raised how often others *checked* it ("It's kind of fun referring my family to [LINC] instead of asking me"), but adding stayed with the mother; pilots took "about two weeks to get into the habit."
- **The cognitive dimension of household labour** (35 couples, 70 interviews; [Daminger, American Sociological Review 84(4) 609-633, 2019](https://inequality.hks.harvard.edu/publications/cognitive-dimension-household-labor), abstract via search): anticipating needs, identifying options, deciding, monitoring; women did more of the anticipating and monitoring, decisions were roughly shared; the work is "often invisible" and a frequent source of conflict.
- **Couples' calendars** (30 Google Calendar users; [Thayer, Bietz, Derthick & Lee, CSCW 2012](https://depts.washington.edu/csclab/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/Thayer-Derthick-Bietz-Lee-2012.pdf), read in full): sharing a calendar is "relationship work"; 20 of 30 split their events across several shared calendars, and wanted flexible views "without the overhead of entering items into separate calendars."
- **[inference]** A board that's easy to *read* doesn't make adding shared. The capture path has to be as cheap for Jenn as for the owner, in habits Jenn already has (Siri, Mail, Photos, the house inbox Jenn already emails), or the board reproduces the primary-scheduler pattern and the load lands on whoever types. The answering-machine trick is "tell the board" before smartphones: a voice drop box reachable from anywhere is the natural shape of capture.

### 1.7 Speech beats thumbs, with one catch

- 32 participants, short phrases: English speech entry was 2.93 times faster than the iPhone keyboard (153 vs 52 words per minute), with fewer errors corrected during entry (5.30% vs 11.22%) but slightly more left in the final text (1.30% vs 0.79%) ([Ruan, Wobbrock, Liou, Ng & Landay, IMWUT 1(4) art. 159, 2017](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1608.07323), via search). A critique questions the comparison ([Language Log](https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=27785)).
- **[inference]** The catch is the uncorrected error: a name, a place or "fourteen" for "forty" survives into the entry. Saying back what landed is the correction step, so it has to be specific.

## 2. AI extraction: how reliable, and how to confirm

### 2.1 Reliability on messy input

- **No public benchmark of current models on household event extraction was found.** The older evidence points one way: reading is the easy part, date arithmetic the weak part.
  - Test of Time (Fatemi et al., [arXiv 2406.09170](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09170v1), 2024-06; ICLR 2025), synthetic time arithmetic, 2024 models (GPT-4, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Claude 3 Sonnet): Duration 13.5 to 16%, Schedule 29.6 to 43.6%, Trick 40.4 to 45.6%, AddSubtract 58.6 to 76.3%, Timezone 74 to 90%. These are puzzles harder than "dentist Tuesday at 3," and the models are two generations old.
  - Relative expressions such as "tomorrow" need an anchor date, the time the text was written ([Lange et al., arXiv 2404.07775, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.07775v1)).
  - Apple's screenshot-to-calendar reading "isn't always accurate," which is why it shows a preview ([Tom's Guide via Yahoo](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/articles/apple-intelligences-best-feature-gains-044500808.html), 2025, via search). A competitor says Ohai's calendar scans are inconsistent ([Carly blog](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/ohai-alternatives/), biased, via search).
- **A 1999 design that still holds.** Microsoft's LookOut read an email and filled in a calendar entry. It took the message's **sent date as the anchor** ("if a message was written yesterday and contains text referring to scheduling a meeting for 'tomorrow,' the system will understand that the message is referring to 'today'"). When it couldn't find a time, it "degrades its goal to identifying a span of time" (a day, a week, a month) and opened that span. "Even when LookOut guesses incorrectly, the user is placed in an approximately correct position" ([Horvitz, CHI 1999, pp. 159-166](https://www.erichorvitz.com/chi99horvitz.pdf), read in full).
- **A 2026 design:** Todoist's Ramble sends raw audio to a Gemini live model that adds, edits and deletes tasks while you speak; tasks show in a preview; "Actually, I meant..." edits one, "that's all" or Add confirms. The team injects the current date and has the model output date phrases in English for Todoist's own deterministic parser. Their lesson: "easy correction beats perfect first-time accuracy in natural language interfaces" ([Product Talk, 2026-04-16](https://www.producttalk.org/building-todoist-ramble-how-doist-turned-voice-braindumps-into-real-time-task-capture/); [Todoist help](https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/use-ramble-to-add-tasks-by-voice-P1Raq7vVF), fetched 2026-10-29).
- **[inference] Rules for this board's Claude path:** give the model today's date and weekday (Home Assistant's date-time tool does) and, for anything shared or forwarded, the message's own date as the anchor; have it return an absolute date and check the weekday matches before writing; always say the weekday and the date back. Repeats: Home Assistant's create action has no repeat field, so the voice path can't make a repeating event today; Family Hub's own create can (section 0), and iOS 27's Siri can add repeating events to iCloud ([MacRumors, 2026-10-05](https://www.macrumors.com/guide/ios-27-calendar-reminders/)). Time zones matter only for travel and online events; models did best on them.

### 2.2 Auto-add with undo, draft-and-confirm, or an inbox to clarify later?

- **Principles.** Horvitz 1999: act only when the expected value beats doing nothing; use "dialog to resolve key uncertainties," weighing "the costs of potentially bothering a user needlessly"; minimize "the cost of poor guesses"; "a preference for 'doing less' but doing it correctly under uncertainty." Amershi et al. ([CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction-design/), 18 guidelines tested by 49 designers on 20 products): G9 "support efficient correction," G10 "scope services when in doubt." Raskin: "as long as it's possible to habituate to dismissing the message, we'll habituate" ([A List Apart, 2007-07-21](https://alistapart.com/article/neveruseawarning)).
- **What products do.** Alexa+: email files to Alexa from your linked address or upload them (JPEG, PNG, HEIC, HEIF, PDF, DOCX, TXT, up to 25 MB); Alexa "searches your shared emails, files and web pages for event names, dates, times, and locations" and proposes events; you can turn on automatic adding to your default calendar; a confirmation email or push lets you "review and add extracted items or remove added items" ([Amazon help, fetched 2026-10-29](https://digprjsurvey.amazon.com/csad/help/node/TVZtvBTaDsIPA1aHyD)). Gemini adds to the default calendar unless told otherwise and can't add guests ([Google help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15305236), fetched via search 2026-10-29). Apple's screenshot button shows an editable preview. Skylight's receipts and drafts are in the ecosystem note.

| Pattern | Fits | Cost per capture | Risk here **[inference]** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Auto-add, say back, undo | One item with an explicit day and time, by voice or share | None beyond listening | A plausible wrong date nobody notices; caught by saying the weekday and date |
| Draft, then confirm | Several items at once (a flyer or schedule), photos | One step | Habituation: people tap Add without reading (Raskin) |
| Inbox to clarify later | Nothing here as a default | Someone must empty it (GTD's weekly review) | Becomes the owner's chore and a second list; Neustaedter's sticky notes were transcribed by the primary scheduler |

- **[inference] Recommendation:** auto-add single items and say back exactly what landed, with undo. When something is missing, add the less precise version (a note on the day, a note on a proposed day this week) rather than ask or reject. Ask at most one question, and only when every guess would be wrong (whose column?). For several items at once, add them all and send one receipt listing each with its own undo (the ecosystem note's Skylight pattern). No separate inbox.

## 3. Things that aren't timed events

- **Products separate "when" from "by":** Todoist's "date" is when you plan to do it, its "deadline" a hard cut-off, and Ramble pulls both out of speech ("Thursday afternoon" vs "must be finished on Friday") ([Todoist help](https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/use-ramble-to-add-tasks-by-voice-P1Raq7vVF)). Fantastical turns a sentence starting "todo", "task" or "reminder" into a reminder ("todo Pick up dry cleaning on Friday") ([The Sweet Setup, 2019](https://thesweetsetup.com/natural-language-guide-for-fantastical/), via search). LookOut understood "sometime tomorrow," "later in the week," "next week," "in May," "at breakfast" and filed each at that span (Horvitz 1999). iOS 27 Reminders fills date, time and place from a typed phrase ([MacRumors, 2026-10-05](https://www.macrumors.com/guide/ios-27-calendar-reminders/)).
- **This board today:** a note lands due today at 9:00 unless a day is given, and rolls over daily; an undated item would be invisible on the week board (section 0).
- **[inference] A ladder that never rejects** (what's said, what lands, where it shows):

| Said | Lands as | Shows |
|---|---|---|
| "dentist tue 3" | Event, Tue 3 to 4 pm, Us | Tuesday's column |
| "call the vet Thursday" | Note due Thursday | Thursday's notes |
| "renew the registration sometime this week" | Note on one proposed day, said back ("I put it on Wednesday") | That day; rolls over from there |
| "passport by Dec 15" | Note due Dec 15, marked as a deadline | "Due in N days" only inside a lead window, not every day before |
| "bring the casserole dish Saturday" | A checklist line on Saturday's event if one matches (Family Hub events take checklists, J008), else a "Bring:" note on Saturday | With that day's events |
| "someday: look at new couches" | Undated item in a Later list, no rollover | Not on the resting board; one tap away |
| Anything unparseable | Note today, with the words as said | Today, and the echo says so |

## 4. Closing the loop

### 4.1 Seeing it land

- The built scripts return an answer that the Shortcut speaks (J006). The ecosystem note (idea 1) and the delight note (idea 6) already argue for saying back title, day, time and column; this lens adds the weekday-and-date check (2.1) and saying what was guessed.
- **[inference]** A failed capture must be loud. If Assist errors or times out, the Shortcut should say "That didn't reach the board" and keep the words (copy them, or save them to a phone note), not speak a cheerful error or nothing.
- **Where undo lives on the phone:** Live Activities can't carry buttons (a tap only opens a page) and expire after up to 8 hours (iOS 17.2+, Home Assistant core 2026.7+, beta; [companion docs](https://companion.home-assistant.io/docs/notifications/live-activities), fetched 2026-10-29), so undo belongs in an actionable notification. iOS hides a notification's actions behind a long press; app 2026.9.3 (2026-09-30) adds an optional, off-by-default setting to show them on tap ([iOS app releases](https://github.com/home-assistant/iOS/releases), fetched 2026-10-29).

### 4.2 How the other partner learns

- In Neustaedter's families, the second adult learned what was on by being told by the primary scheduler or by glancing at the public calendar; home reminders failed when they reached you only at a computer (TOCHI 2009).
- iCloud shared calendars tell other members about changes, with a per-calendar "Show Changes" switch, delivered in the app or by email for the whole Apple Account ([Apple iPod touch guide](https://support.apple.com/en-il/guide/ipod-touch/iph7613c4fb/ios); [iCloud guide](https://support.apple.com/en-bh/guide/icloud/mm6b1a9479/icloud), via search). The Home Assistant "Us" calendar has nothing like it.
- **[inference]** Default to ambient: the board itself, plus the ecosystem note's "new since you last looked" dot (its idea 4), plus the spoken echo when both are in the room. Push only when the item asks something of the other person ("for both of us," "ask Jenn to..."), once, at capture. That fits the house rule to push as rarely as is defensible.

## 7. Recommended capture flows for this household  [inference]

Each flow uses the fewest new pieces. "Board calendar" means whichever shared iCloud calendar the owner makes the phones' default (idea 1).

- **A text.** If the date is underlined, tap it, then Add: it lands on the board calendar. If not, share the text to "Tell the board" (idea 4), or on iOS 27 with Siri AI say "Hey Siri, add this to my calendar" with the message on screen. For a messy text with an address and a time, take a screenshot and tap Add to Calendar.
- **An email.** One clear appointment (a booking confirmation): use Mail's suggestion or tap the date. An email with several dates or a PDF (a vet's vaccine reminders, a newsletter): forward it to the board address once that exists (idea 9), and read the one receipt that comes back. Anything about the house itself still goes to the house inbox, which already files it. Invitations: accept them into the board calendar.
- **A paper flyer, appointment card or postcard.** On iOS 27 with an iPhone 15 Pro or later: the camera's Siri mode, review, add, recycle the paper; the board holds it now. On an older phone: read it out to "tell the board," or photograph it and forward it once email-in exists.
- **A phone call.** Say it right after hanging up ("tell the board, vet Thursday at 4"). Most offices send a confirmation text or email; tapping its date is the fallback that fixes a misheard time. Don't record calls to capture them.
- **A conversation, at home or in the car.** Whoever's hands are free says it: "Hey Siri, tell the board, dinner with the neighbours Saturday at 7." Both people hear the echo, which is the partner's notification. In the car, Siri through CarPlay with the same phrase.
- **A thought on the go.** "Tell the board, call the vet sometime this week": it lands on a proposed day and says which. "Someday" ideas go to the Later list, off the daily view (idea 5). For a dreaded call, accept the slot it proposes (idea 6).
- **At the wall.** Changing or clearing is the wall's job: tap the item, then Done, Tomorrow or Remove, each with Undo (delight note). Adding at the wall is for whoever is already standing there: one line of rough text through the same tidy-up path as voice (idea 12), or the phone in hand.

## 9. What this lens says not to do

- **Don't ask for anything at capture that can be defaulted.** No required time, duration, person or calendar; no "which calendar?" picker. Every extra decision is paid in captures not made (Chiu & Gilbert), and for a dreaded item the decision is the aversive part (Steel; Sirois & Pychyl).
- **Don't reject rough input.** The worst case is a note today with the words as said, and the echo says so (Horvitz principle 8).
- **Don't build an inbox someone must process.** It becomes the owner's weekly chore and a second list nobody trusts; GTD needs a weekly review, and Neustaedter's sticky notes waited for the primary scheduler.
- **Don't confirm with "Are you sure?"** Add, say what landed, offer undo (Raskin; delight note).
- **Don't let a failure be silent.** One lost capture teaches both adults to check, which doubles the cost of every later one (Weis & Wiese).
- **Don't let the model do date arithmetic unchecked,** and don't anchor a forwarded or shared message to today's date (Test of Time; LookOut).
- **Don't scan mailboxes or messages on your own.** Capture is something a person sends on purpose; work mail and anything sensitive stay out, and the house bot's screen for sensitive content is the floor for any email path.
- **Don't nag.** No daily push for rolled-over notes, no red overdue counts, no streaks; a board people avoid looking at stops being glanced at (ostrich problem; the house rule on reminders).
- **Don't add phrases.** One pair, "tell the board" and "ask the board," for everything (J008 Q10); a second vocabulary is something to remember.
- **Don't build the floor on beta or Labs features.** Siri AI (beta), Live Activities (beta) and Reminders Sync (Labs) are welcome extras; the built voice path and the phones' own calendars are the floor.
- **Don't make capture an owner-only skill.** Every path in section 7 works the same on Jenn's phone, or it isn't the recommended path (Daminger; Neustaedter).

## 10. The sentence the synthesis must not lose

Capture has to cost one gesture or one sentence from wherever the obligation shows up, accept it in whatever rough shape it arrives, and say back exactly what landed where with an undo, because "it's handled" is only true when both adults trust the board to have it.
